About the Project

This project analyzes the textual content of a news article and predicts whether it is likely to be Real or Fake, using a machine-learning model trained on labeled news examples. It is intended as an educational demonstration of applying Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Machine Learning to the problem of misinformation detection.

What is Fake News?

Fake news refers to false or misleading information presented as legitimate news content. It can be spread intentionally to deceive readers or unintentionally through the rapid sharing of unverified claims, particularly on social media platforms.

Why Fake News Detection Matters

The rapid growth of digital media and social networks has made it easier for misinformation to spread quickly and widely. Fake news can influence public opinion, damage reputations, and interfere with informed decision-making. Automated detection tools can help flag potentially misleading content for further human review.

Machine Learning Workflow

News Text → Text Preprocessing → TF-IDF → Logistic Regression → Prediction → Confidence Score

The confidence score reflects the probability the trained model assigns to its predicted class. A higher score means the model's learned patterns more strongly match the submitted text — it is a measure of model certainty, not a guarantee of factual accuracy.

Limitations
  1. The model depends on the quality and coverage of its training dataset.
  2. The system analyzes textual patterns rather than independently verifying facts.
  3. Confidence scores represent model confidence, not factual certainty.
  4. Newly emerging topics may not be well represented in the training data.
  5. The system should not replace professional fact-checking or trusted news sources.
